Give too little detail, and you will receive a vague estimate that could skyrocket later. Give too much or say the wrong things, and you might lose your competitive edge. Finding the perfect balance ensures you get an accurate, fair, and competitive bid.

* What You Should Share: The Specifics of the Vision

Detailed Scope of Work: Clearly outline what is staying, what is going, and what needs to be built from scratch.

Measurements and Layouts: If you have architectural drawings or basic sketches with dimensions, share them.

Material Expectations: You don't need to choose exact paint codes, but specify the tier of finishes you expect (e.g., custom hardwood versus laminate flooring, or quartz versus laminate countertops).

Site Conditions: Let them know about any known structural quirks, access limitations, or HOA regulations they will need to navigate.

* What You Should Not Share: Your Hand

While transparency builds trust, disclosing certain strategic variables can inadvertently hurt your negotiating position:

Your Maximum Budget: Avoid stating your exact budget limit upfront. If you tell a contractor you have $50,000 to spend, a bid that might have cost $42,000 can easily creep up to match your maximum cap. Instead, ask for an itemized cost breakdown based purely on the work required.

If They Are the Only Bidder: Always let contractors know you are gathering multiple estimates. If they think they are your only option, they have less incentive to give you a highly competitive price.

Too Much Scheduling Flexibility: Even if you aren't in a massive rush, telling a contractor your timeline is "completely wide open" can push your project to the bottom of their priority list. Provide a realistic target start and end date to keep the project moving forward.

The Bottom Line

Think of the initial quote request as a roadmap. Show the contractor exactly where you want to go and what materials you want to use to get there, but let them tell you how much it costs to make the trip. By providing strict project parameters while keeping your budget and bidding strategy private, you protect your wallet and set your project up for success.
